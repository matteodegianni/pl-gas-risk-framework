# Management memo - gas supply risk

To: CFO and Management Board | As of 2026-11-06 | **Illustrative: synthetic book, limits and counterparties on real
market prices.** All figures are generated by the risk models (`python -m gasrisk.memo`).

**Recommendation.** Hedge with **TTF futures at 70% of open volume**, with the EUR forward. Over
2,300 historical 21-day periods this cuts the worst-case (99%) loss by 45.2%, against
44.2% at the statistically optimal ratio of 0.900 and
42.4% with an options collar. The higher ratio adds no tail protection but
would raise margin needs by 29%; the collar costs
1,012,148 PLN in option value and protects less.

**Cost of risk.** Futures carry no premium; the cost is liquidity. Holding the margin buffer of
7,085,393 EUR costs about 184,269 EUR a year at the current euro rate
(2.60%). Today's one-day 99% value at risk is 18,323,060 PLN,
73% of the 25,000,000 PLN limit.

**Margin and bank line.** Exchange initial margin is 3,676,450 EUR. In 99% of 10-day periods the
extra cash call stays below 7,085,393 EUR (31,122,589 PLN),
28% of the 25,000,000 EUR bank line. The
worst stress, a repeat of the 2022-03-08 to 2022-03-21 TTF collapse, would call 17,864,382 EUR,
71% of the line. Margin risk comes when gas prices **fall**, the opposite of
the P&L risk. At 2022 price levels the same hedge would have needed 1.7x
the line in 10 days: the line should be reviewed if prices rise.

**Key risks.**
1. **Polish price spikes.** The Dutch TTF hedge does not follow Polish day-ahead spikes. The worst historical
   scenario would lose 689,596,369 PLN, 38 times today's
   value at risk; in crisis periods the hedge cuts the worst-case loss by only 12%.
   This basis explains 99% of the
   hedge's misses.
2. **Hedge accounting.** The hedge passes the month-by-month offset test in only 29% of
   months, so reported P&L would stay volatile under hedge accounting without a Polish-price hedge.
3. **Exposure limits.** Higher gas prices have pushed open gas exposure to 102%
   and the FX open position to 116% of their limits.
4. **Counterparty.** CP04, the weakest-rated counterparty, uses 14 times its exposure
   limit; exposures to gas sellers rise when prices spike, when they are weakest.

**Decisions requested.** (1) Approve the 70% futures hedge policy. (2) Approve a plan for the two exposure-limit
breaches. (3) Require collateral or a lower tenor from CP04. (4) Ask treasury to confirm the bank line covers the
17,864,382 EUR stress call.

Contract P&L since signing: 1,565,811 PLN against a plan of 10,000,000 PLN.
