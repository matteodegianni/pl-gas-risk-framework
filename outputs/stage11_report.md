**Dollar-offset fails**: only 29.1% of 110 periods fall inside
80%-125% with TTF futures alone; the cumulative ratio over
rolling 12-period (one-year) relationships passes in 40.4% of them.
60.3% of the failing periods have a TGE change below the median absolute change, the
known small-denominator weakness of the ratio. The regression test **passes** (slope 1.1143, R2 0.8439 against a minimum of 0.8): over many periods TTF explains most of the TGE move, but period by period the basis noise is too large for the dollar-offset band. The two tests disagree, which is common for cross hedges; the regression test is the more stable one.

### Where the ineffectiveness comes from

Exact identity per MWh: dS - dF = dB_adj x FX_t + TGE_EUR_t-1 x dFX, with dB_adj the roll-adjusted change of the
TGE-TTF location plus tenor basis.

- The basis term carries **99.1%** of the variance of the ineffectiveness (covariance

- In failing periods the basis term is 93.4% of the
  absolute ineffectiveness (49.8% in passing periods).
- The failure is therefore the Stage 8 finding seen from the accounting side: the TGE day-ahead vs TTF second-month

  would be needed to pass; neither is in the free data.

## Limitations

- Simplified effectiveness tests, not IFRS 9 (no designation documentation, no hypothetical derivative).
- Collar values use the Stage 10 volatility assumption with no smile; the option tenor (Jan 2027) is held for 21 days.
- Strategy comparison replays historical joint moves on today's levels; transaction costs and margin funding are not
  included (margin in Stage 12).
